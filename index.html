✨ The Mystery of the 22 Shrutis (श्रुति)

In ancient treatises like Bharata Muni’s Natya Shastra and Sarangadeva’s Sangita Ratnakara, an octave is divided into 22 microtonal intervals called Shrutis. Unlike Western 12-TET equal temperament, Indian music uses just intonation (pure mathematical harmonics). A Komal Gandhara (flat Ga) in Raga Todi sits at a different microtonal shade than in Raga Darbari. This microtonal precision is why Indian classical music evokes deep, nuanced human emotion.

The Time Theory of Ragas

One of the most extraordinary achievements of Indian Classical Music is its synchronization with the human circadian rhythm. The day is divided into eight 3-hour periods (Prahars), each commanding specific notes and acoustic resonances.

Acoustic Wonders • वाद्य वृन्द

Treasury of Classical Instruments

Instruments engineered specifically to mimic the organic microtones, vocal inflections, and emotional nuances of the human throat.

🪕

Tanpura (Tambura)

Drone Foundation • श्रुति धारक

The non-negotiable soul of every concert. Four or five metal strings tuned to Pa, Sa, Sa', and low Sa vibrate continuously over a bone bridge, producing a rich, enveloping acoustic ocean of overtone harmonics.

Gourd: Dried Bottle Gourd (Tumba) Strings: 4 Brass & Steel Purpose: Pure harmonic pitch anchor
🥁

Tabla & Dagga

Percussive Maestro • ताल नायक

A pair of twin drums: the wooden Dayan (treble drum tuned to Sa) and the metal or clay Bayan (bass drum capable of expressive pitch modulation with the wrist heel). The black circular iron paste (*Syahi*) creates bell-like overtones.

Heads: Multi-layered goat leather Center: Iron & rice starch paste (Syahi) Modulation: Microtonal bass slides
🎵

Sitar

Melodic Plucked Lute • तंत्र वाद्य

Featuring curved movable frets and sympathetic resonance strings (*Tarab*), the Sitar allows performers to pull strings sideways across frets, producing glides (*Meend*) up to 5 full notes from a single pluck.

Main Strings: 6 or 7 Sympathetic Strings: 11 to 13 Plectrum: Wire Mizrab worn on finger
🎋

Bansuri (Flute)

Sacred Bamboo Breath • सुषिर वाद्य

A simple hollow bamboo cane with six finger holes, yet capable of extraordinary expressiveness. By partially venting finger holes and tilting the embouchure, the flutist crafts every delicate gamaka and shruti.

Material: Special aged Assam bamboo Range: Over 2.5 octaves Iconic Maestro: Pt. Hariprasad Chaurasia
🎸

Sarod

Fretless Plucked Lute • स्वरोद

With a fretless polished steel fingerboard and goat-skin soundboard, the Sarod yields deep, resonant, and lightning-fast glissandi. The strings are pressed with the fingernails rather than fingertips.

Soundboard: Stretched goatskin Plectrum: Coconut shell (Jawa) Legacy: Senia & Maihar Gharanas
🎹

Harmonium

Vocal Companion • संवादिनी

A free-reed keyboard instrument pumped by hand bellows. In Indian vocal music, the harmonium provides a steadfast contour, supporting the vocalist’s melodic phrases and ornamentation.

Type: Bellow-driven brass reeds Role: Vocal accompaniment & accompaniment Features: Drone stops, scale changers
Two Streams, One Ocean • संगीत धारा

Hindustani vs. Carnatic Tradition

Around the 13th century, Indian classical music bifurcated into the Northern (Hindustani) and Southern (Carnatic) streams, each preserving ancient Vedic roots while developing distinct expressive architectures.

Dimension Hindustani Music (North) Carnatic Music (South)
Geography & Influence Northern & Central India; influenced by Persian, Central Asian, and Sufi interaction. Southern India (Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Kerala, Andhra); retains deep temple and Vedic traditions.
Structural System Organized into 10 Thaats (Pt. Bhatkhande) with strong improvisation freedom. Organized into the rigorous 72 Melakarta parent scale system (Venkatamakhin).
Time of Day Rigor Strictly tied to the Samay Chakra (morning, evening, night ragas). Less emphasis on time of day; focus is on compositional devotion (*Bhakti*).
Major Performance Forms Khayal, Dhrupad, Tarana, Thumri, Dadra. Kriti, Varnam, Ragam-Tanam-Pallavi (RTP), Tillana.
Key Instruments Sitar, Sarod, Santoor, Bansuri, Tabla, Harmonium. Saraswati Veena, Venu flute, Violin, Mridangam, Ghatam.
Pedagogy Heritage Gharana system (e.g. Gwalior, Kirana, Agra, Jaipur-Atrauli). Guru-Shishya parampara anchored by the Trinity (Tyagaraja, Dikshitar, Sastri).
The Discipline of Mastery • साधना

The Art of Daily Riyaz

Riyaz is not mere practice; it is a sacred meditation where the student surrenders to the pitch and achieves acoustic stillness (*Chitta Shuddhi*).

01

Kharaj Sadhana

Early morning practice in the Mandra Saptak (lower octave). Singing low Sa, Ni, Dha for prolonged breaths expands lung capacity, stabilizes vocal cords, and deepens timbre.

02

Swara Sthirata

Holding pure notes with zero vibrato against a steady Tanpura drone. The goal is to merge your voice with the drone until all acoustic beats disappear into resonance.

03

Alankars & Paltas

Mathematical permutations of notes (e.g., SaReGa, ReGaMa, GaMaPa). Practicing these across three octaves at varying speeds (Vilambit, Madhyalaya, Drut) trains muscle memory.

04

Laya & Tala Precision

Practicing with steady rhythm accompaniment to land effortlessly on the Sam. Mastery over rhythm ensures confidence in complex improvisations.
